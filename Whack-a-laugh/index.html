Can You Keep the Silence at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier?

Everyday since 1937, a Sentinel from the Army’s 3rd U.S. Infantry Regiment, the oldest active-duty unit in the U.S. army, has walked the mat in front of the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier. Signs on the plaza ask the crowd for one thing — silence and respect — and when visitors forget, the Sentinel will stop the walk and remind them out loud.

In this game, you are that Sentinel. Tap the visitors who laugh, chat, take phone calls or try to get a closer angle, but leave the quiet vistors and photographers alone. Watch for the VIP who thinks this is a joke: he’s slippery, and silencing him is worth big points. Hold the crowd for 60-seconds.

At the Tomb

    About the Tomb

    The Tomb of the Unknown Soldier sits on a hill at Arlington National Cemetery, overlooking Washington, D.C. Its west face reads: “Here rests in honored glory an American soldier known but to God.”

    Soldiers of the 3rd U.S. Infantry Regiment — “The Old Guard” — have guarded it every minute of every day since 1937, in every kind of weather. Signs on the plaza ask visitors for silence and respect, and when a crowd forgets, the Sentinel will stop the walk and remind them.

    This game is a lighthearted look at that duty. The real place is a solemn one: if you visit, stay quiet and stay behind the chains.

    Sources: Arlington National Cemetery; 3rd U.S. Infantry Regiment (The Old Guard).
